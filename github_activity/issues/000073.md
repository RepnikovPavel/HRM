The paper does not mention the Sparse Embedding trained with SignSGD. The code says "Adam ≈ SignSGD if gradient is very sparse", but why would the embedding of the puzzle-id be sparse? And why not use Adam if its approximately equivalent to SignSGD when the embedding is sparse?  

Could you share a paper that document the benefit of SignSGD for embeddings or was this found to slightly improve performance from hyperparameter-tuning?


## Comments (2)

### Comment by @imoneoi at 2025-09-08T14:19:57Z

The paper mentioned that we prepended a special token as puzzle-id embedding. As puzzle-id is randomly sampled, and the embedding size is large compared to batch size, making it sparse. Thus, AdamW momentums are near zero, essentially reducing to SignSGD. Directly implementing as SignSGD saves compute and VRAM.
